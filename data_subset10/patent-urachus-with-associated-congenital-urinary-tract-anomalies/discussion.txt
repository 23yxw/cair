Patent
urachus
is an uncommon congenital anomaly caused by complete failure of obliteration of the embryonic urachus, resulting in persistent communication between the
urinary bladder
and
umbilicus
. It typically presents during childhood with continuous or intermittent urinary discharge from the umbilicus.
In this case, post-micturition CT directly demonstrates contrast passage through a patent tract from the urinary bladder towards the umbilicus, confirming the diagnosis. MRI further delineates the urachal tract and associated pelvic anatomical abnormality. Marked
diastasis
of the pubic symphysis, with the urinary bladder positioned between the separated pubic bones, together with clinically evident epispadias and dorsal chordee, raises the possibility of an associated anomaly within the genitourinary epispadias spectrum.
Additional congenital urinary tract anomalies include a
horseshoe kidney
with lower pole fusion and partial duplication of the left collecting system with distal ureteric fusion. Bilateral bowel-containing
inguinal hernias
are also present without obstruction or incarceration.
This case highlights the importance of comprehensive assessment of the urinary tract, kidneys, collecting systems and pelvic anatomy in children presenting with urinary discharge from the umbilicus, as a patent urachus may occur in association with complex congenital genitourinary and pelvic anomalies.
