Spondylolisthesis
can be classified based by etiology (
Wiltse classification
) or degree of slippage (
Meyerding classification
).
In the present case, there are mixed features of both isthmic and degenerative spondylolisthesis. While pars defect is the mainstay of isthmic-type spondylolisthesis, progression is rare and usually happens in early adulthood. Degenerative-type spondylolisthesis is much more common at the L4-L5 level, but it also happens in other levels.
Dynamic imaging of the spine (flexion and extension views) is useful for evaluating instability. In this case, anterior L5 translation >3 mm on flexion lateral indicates an unstable L5-S1 segment.
The
SDSG classification
is the most useful to plan adequate management. This case can be classified as type 4: high-grade (>50%) L5-S1 slippage and balanced pelvis (high sacral slope and low pelvic tilt). Spinal instrumentation with in situ fixation is recommended for such cases.
