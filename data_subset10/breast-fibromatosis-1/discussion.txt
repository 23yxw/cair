Fibromatosis or desmoid
tumor of the breast is a rare benign lesion (0.2% of breast tumors). It consists of a connective tissue growth that, although benign, is locally aggressive.
Histology: Proliferation of spindle-shaped cells (fibroblasts and myofibroblasts) in the breast connective tissue in long fascicles, with finger-like projections that infiltrate the breast and adipose tissue. It lacks significant nuclear atypia, prominent mitoses, or necrosis. Immunohistochemically, nuclear positivity for β-catenin.
Clinical presentation: It presents as a palpable, firm, and adherent mass with poorly defined borders, which may cause retraction of the skin or pectoral muscle.
Associated factors: It has been linked to previous trauma, surgery, breast implants, or Gardner syndrome, although its exact cause is unknown.
Treatment: Although benign, it is locally aggressive and requires surgical excision with wide margins due to its high recurrence rate.
