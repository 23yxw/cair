Slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE)
, also known as slipped upper femoral epiphysis (SUFE), refers to the failure of the proximal femoral physis, usually atraumatic, with consequent displacement of the epiphysis relative to the femoral neck.
SCFE is most common in boys between 12 and 15 years old. When affecting girls, such as in this case, it happens earlier, between 11 and 13 years old, and rarely after menarche.
Prodromal symptoms of vague groin, thigh, or hip pain are common and represent the pre-slip stage.
SCFE can be classified in many ways. One of the most important is regarding stability. If the patient is unable to bear weight, the SCFE is unstable. This classification is relevant to management, which includes avoiding frog-leg lateral views before reduction due to the risk of contributing to
osteonecrosis of the femoral head
.
Regarding severity, different classifications may be used. In the present case, there is >60° of displacement of the right femoral epiphysis compared to the left one, which can be classified as a severe slip according to the Southwick classification.
In this case, the patient underwent surgical dislocation and subcapital osteotomy on the right hip. The surgical approach requires osteotomy of the greater trochanter, which needs to be fixed during closure (hence the smaller screws).
Contralateral prophylactic fixation is a matter of debate in the literature, but it is recognized that potential growth is the main risk factor for contralateral SCFE manifestation. In this case, in situ percutaneous fixation was done prophylactically for the left hip.
